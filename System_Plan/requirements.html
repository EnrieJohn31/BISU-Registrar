What we need from the offices

Requirements checklist for the BISU Registrar system, Balilihan Campus · Version 2, 18 October 2026 (replaces version 1 of 17 October)

How to use this list

  • It is grouped by who to ask: Registrar, Cashier, MIS, the Data Protection Officer, the adviser, and our own team.
  • Each item says what to ask in plain words and why we need it, with an example answer (green) showing the kind of reply that helps.
  • The tag on the right says when: First blocks the next step, Soon before the first full request, Later for reports and extras.

What we already have

  • One page of the Citizen's Charter (page 115, Enrollment). It shows how each service is described, but not the steps, fees or processing time.
  • Working with made-up data: staff sign-in and roles, an activity log, and client records. The login page also opens on a phone over Wi-Fi.
  • Ready to use: the baseline kit for counting clients and waiting times, and the design of the services and fees screens.
Ground rules for every request
  • Never ask for or carry real student or alumni records, payment details or ID numbers. Ask for blank forms and made-up examples only.
  • The green examples are invented. They show the kind of answer we need. They are not BISU's rules, so never copy one as an answer.
  • Ask for the official, approved version of each document, not someone's memory of it.
  • For every answer, write down who said it (name and position), the date, and the document name and page. Clear photos or scans are fine if they show no personal data.

1 The rest of the Citizen's Charter (2024, 1st Edition): the whole Registration Office section, especially the pages after page 115 that show each service's steps, fees, processing time and person responsible. Why: the services screens copy the charter's layout. We have only the Enrollment page. Ref D05 "Here are clear photos of pages 116 to 130, the whole section 5.2."First

3 Which office name should clients see on screens? The charter says "Registration Office"; we have been saying "Registrar's Office". Why: the name appears on every screen and printout. Ref D05 "Use 'Office of the Registrar'. 'Registration Office' is the old charter wording."First
4 Which documents and services should the system handle first, and which are requested most? Also: is Enrollment part of this system, or does it stay outside? Why: we build the most-used services first and leave out what the office handles elsewhere. Ref D01 "Start with a certification, a transcript request and authentication. Certifications are most requested, about 20 a day. Enrollment stays outside this system."First
5 For each service, what must the client bring or fill in? If it differs by client (current student, alumni, transferee), list each group, as page 115 does. For each item say whether it can be uploaded as a photo or must be shown at the counter, and whether a clearance from another office (library, accounting) is needed. Why: the request form asks for exactly these things. Ref D05 "Certification: current students fill in the request form and show their school ID at the counter. Alumni also show a government ID. A representative brings an authorization letter. Library clearance only for transcripts; the clerk checks the signed slip."First
6 What do Student IDs and Alumni IDs look like? Length, letters or numbers, dashes, leading zeros, whether an ID is ever reused, and whether alumni keep their student ID. Give made-up examples. Why: the system currently uses a placeholder rule and refuses real IDs until we know. Ref D04 "Student IDs are the entry year, a dash, then four digits, like 2099-0042. Zeros at the front are kept. IDs are never reused. Alumni keep their student ID."First
7 The official student and alumni list: who keeps it, its column headings (a blank copy with headings only), how often it changes, and who fixes duplicates and mistakes. Why: the system loads this list so that it knows who may request documents. Ref D04 "The records clerk keeps it in Excel: ID, last name, first name, middle name, program, year graduated. Updated each semester. The Registrar decides on duplicates."First
8 How do you make sure a person is really that student or alumnus? And what should happen when someone forgets their password or loses access? Why: before clients can request online, their account must be linked to the right person. Ref D03 "They show a school or government ID at the counter and staff compare the name and photo. If they lose access, they come to the office with an ID."First
9 One contact person who can answer our questions, and who in the office approves rules and will test the system later. Why: every answer needs an owner, and testing needs real staff. Ref D14 "Ask the records clerk first. The Registrar approves. Two clerks can test in March."First
10 How do clients line up today? Office hours and days, lunch break, how senior citizens, persons with disabilities and pregnant women are served, and what happens if a called client is not there. Why: the queue screens follow these rules. The baseline count also measures them. Ref D06 "8:00 to 5:00, Monday to Friday, open over lunch. One line. Seniors and PWDs go first at window 1. We call three times, then serve the next person."Soon
11 Blank copies of each document the office prints (certifications and similar), who signs each one, how they are numbered, the paper size, any special paper, and the printer brand and model. Is approval a wet signature? Why: we tested making certificates with fake layouts. We must test again with the real blank layout and printer. Ref D08 "Certification on A4 security paper, signed by the Registrar, numbered by year (2026-0001). Printer: an office laser printer, model on the label. Wet signature."Soon
12 Who may claim a document? Can a relative or friend claim it, and what must they bring? Why: the release screen checks this before handing over a document. Ref D08 "Yes, with an authorization letter and both people's IDs. For transcripts, a notarized SPA."Soon
13 How long may a request wait for missing papers or payment before it is closed? What happens to documents nobody claims, and after how many days? Why: the system sends reminders and lists old requests, but never closes them on its own. Ref D18 "Incomplete requests close after 30 days, with a reminder after 7. Unclaimed documents are kept for 6 months, then filed."Soon
14 The academic calendar and holiday list, and how class or work suspensions are announced. Why: processing days are counted in working days, and the queue closes on suspended days. Ref D06, D12 "The calendar is a PDF from the university. Suspensions are posted on the BISU Facebook page by the President's office."Soon
15 Blank samples of the reports made today (daily, monthly, semester, academic year) and who receives each one. Why: the system's reports should match what the office already submits. Ref D12 "A monthly report of documents issued, in Excel, to the Campus Director by the 5th."Later
16 How is client feedback collected today (the paper satisfaction form)? Who compiles the report? Which languages should clients see on screen? Why: feedback and language options come at the end of the build. Ref D19, D01 "Paper form at the window, compiled monthly by the clerk. English on screens is fine; Cebuano would be nice later."Later
17 Supplier list: how suppliers should be rated, how much each point counts, the score that should trigger a review, how often scores reset, and which supplier details and documents to keep. Why: the supplier list is built after the main request flow. Ref D15, D16 "On-time delivery 40%, quality 40%, complete papers 20%. Review anyone below 70. Reset every year. Keep the business permit expiry date."Later
18 Sign the baseline permission form (page 2 of the baseline kit), so the team may count arrivals and time service at the counter for about five days, with no names. Why: it gives the thesis a "before" picture to compare with later. Ref OF-4 "Signed. Observe 21 to 25 October, 9 to 10 and 2 to 3. Stand near the bench by the door. Avoid Friday afternoon."First

Cashier's Office items 19–25

19 The fee for each service, the document that approved it (board resolution or fee schedule) and since when. Is it per copy, per page or per request? Does it differ by client group? Why: the system computes what a client owes. It must never guess an amount. Ref D05, D07 "Certification: ₱__ per copy. Transcript: ₱__ per page. Both from Board Resolution No. __, series of 20__, effective June 2024. Same for everyone."First
20 Are some clients exempt from fees or given discounts? Who approves it, and what proof is needed? Why: a fee waiver needs its own approval step, separate from a free service. Ref D05, D07 "No discounts. Graduating students get their first certification free, with the Campus Director's approval."First
21 The Cashier's section of the Citizen's Charter: payment steps, processing time and person responsible. Why: the payment part of the request follows it. Ref D05 "Section 5.4, pages 140 to 142. Photos attached."First
22 How can clients pay (cash, bank deposit, e-wallet)? How are official receipts issued and numbered? Who records a payment and who checks it? Why: the Cashier screen records the receipt number and who checked it. Ref D07 "Cash at the window only. Manual receipt booklets, numbered by series. The cashier issues the receipt; the collecting officer checks the day's receipts at closing."First
23 Partial payments, overpayments and refunds: are they allowed, and who approves them? Why: these need separate records, never edits to a payment. Ref D07 "Full payment only. Overpayments are returned on the spot. Refunds are approved by the Accountant."Soon
24 The daily closing time for payments, and a blank copy of the daily collection report (for example the Report of Collections and Deposits). Why: the system's totals must match the official report. Ref D07, D12 "Payments until 4:00 p.m. The report is prepared daily and given to Accounting the next morning. Blank copy attached."Soon
25 Sign the baseline permission form for the Cashier's counter (page 2 of the baseline kit). Why: same as item 18, for the payment window. Ref OF-4 "Signed. Observe in the mornings only. Do not stand behind the window."First
