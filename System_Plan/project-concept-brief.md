PROJECT CONCEPT BRIEF

Proposed Project Title

Integrated Registrar–Cashier Document Request, Queuing, and Transaction Management System for Bohol Island State University – Balilihan Campus

In short BISU Registrar is the title

I. Background and Rationale

Bohol Island State University – Balilihan Campus continues to improve the delivery of administrative and student services through digital transformation and process improvement. However, several transactions involving the Registrar’s Office and Cashier’s Office still rely on manual or partially digitized processes, particularly in document requests, queuing, payment verification, transaction monitoring, and report generation.

As the Information Technology (IT) program serves as one of the flagship academic programs of the campus, there is an opportunity to further strengthen the campus’ culture of innovation and digital transformation by developing and implementing practical information systems that address actual institutional needs.

At present, there remains an opportunity for the campus to increase the number of institutional systems and digital solutions developed and implemented locally, particularly systems that can directly improve frontline services. The proposed project may therefore serve as a practical avenue for IT students to apply their knowledge and skills in software development, database management, systems analysis and design, information security, and user experience while providing a meaningful solution to an actual campus operational requirement.

The proposed system will integrate the transaction workflows of the Registrar’s Office and Cashier’s Office into a centralized platform. Students and alumni may initiate transactions using their Student ID or Alumni ID, while authorized personnel can manage requests, queues, payments, document processing, releases, and reports through their respective system interfaces.

II. General Objective

To develop an integrated information system that will streamline and digitize document requests, queuing, payment-related transactions, processing, and reporting of the Registrar’s and Cashier’s Offices of BISU – Balilihan Campus.

III. Specific Objectives

The proposed system aims to:

1. Provide a centralized platform for student and alumni requests for Registrar-related services;
2. Enable identification of clients using their Student ID or Alumni ID;
3. Establish an organized queuing mechanism for campus frontline services;
4. Integrate Registrar and Cashier transaction workflows;
5. Facilitate payment verification for transactions requiring fees;
6. Automate the generation of commonly requested forms, certifications, and applicable documents;
7. Provide real-time monitoring of transaction status from request to completion or release;
8. Generate statistical and operational reports on transactions, services, and documents requested;
9. Provide role-based access to ensure that users can only access information and functions appropriate to their responsibilities; and
10. Maintain an audit trail of important system activities for accountability and monitoring.

IV. Proposed System Modules

A. Student/Alumni Portal

* Student/Alumni ID identification
* Profile verification
* Document/service selection
* Request submission
* Queue number generation
* Request status monitoring
* Transaction history

B. Registrar Module

* Request management
* Record verification
* Document processing
* Queue management
* Document/form generation
* Approval and release monitoring
* Transaction reports

C. Cashier Module

* Payment-related transaction monitoring
* Payment verification/recording
* Transaction status updating
* Collection-related reports
* Payment transaction history

D. Document Generation Module

* Standardized certifications
* Request forms
* Authentication-related forms
* CAV-related processing documents
* Other commonly used Registrar forms/documents, subject to existing university policies and approved templates

E. Reporting and Analytics Module

* Daily transaction reports
* Monthly transaction reports
* Semester reports
* Academic Year reports
* Number of clients served
* Most requested documents/services
* Transaction status reports
* Registrar transaction statistics
* Cashier/payment-related statistics

F. User and Security Management

* Role-based access control
* User account management
* Activity/audit logs
* Secure handling of student and alumni information
* Data privacy and authorization controls

V. Proposed Workflow

Student/Alumni

→ Identify using Student/Alumni ID
→ Select requested service/document
→ Submit request
→ Receive queue number
→ Monitor request status

Registrar

→ Receive request
→ Verify client/records
→ Process document
→ If payment is required → Endorse transaction to Cashier

Cashier

→ Receive payment transaction
→ Verify/record payment
→ Update payment status

Registrar

→ Continue document processing
→ Generate/approve document
→ Mark as Ready for Release

Student/Alumni

→ Receive/claim completed document
→ Transaction marked as Completed

VI. Expected Benefits

The implementation of the proposed system is expected to:

* Reduce manual encoding and repetitive data entry;
* Improve the organization of client queues;
* Reduce unnecessary movement between offices;
* Improve coordination between the Registrar and Cashier;
* Provide faster monitoring of pending and completed transactions;
* Standardize commonly used forms and documents;
* Improve the accuracy and availability of operational reports;
* Provide management with data that can support planning and decision-making;
* Improve the overall student and alumni service experience; and
* Contribute to the digital transformation and institutionalization of IT-based solutions within the campus.

VII. Potential Contribution to the IT Program

As the IT program is one of the flagship programs of BISU – Balilihan Campus, the project can demonstrate the practical contribution of the program beyond classroom instruction.

The project may serve as a living institutional application of the competencies acquired by IT students, particularly in systems analysis and design, software development, database management, cybersecurity, information systems, and project management.

More importantly, successful implementation may establish a model for future thesis and capstone projects where IT students develop solutions based on actual institutional problems and requirements, thereby increasing the number of locally developed systems that can be adopted and utilized by the campus.

VIII. Proposed Collaboration

The project may be undertaken by qualified IT thesis/capstone students in coordination with:

* IT Thesis Coordinator;
* IT faculty/advisers;
* Registrar’s Office;
* Cashier’s Office;
* Campus MIS/IT personnel; and
* Other concerned offices, as necessary.

The concerned offices may provide process documentation, workflow information, approved forms/templates, and other non-restricted references necessary for requirements gathering and system development.

Access to student, alumni, academic, and financial information shall be strictly subject to BISU policies, applicable data privacy regulations, information security requirements, and proper authorization.

IX. Expected Project Output

Upon completion, the proponents may be expected to deliver:

1. Functional web-based or campus-approved information system;
2. Centralized transaction database;
3. Student/Alumni request interface;
4. Registrar and Cashier management dashboards;
5. Queuing management functionality;
6. Document/form generation functionality;
7. Reporting and analytics dashboard;
8. User and access management;
9. Audit trail functionality;
10. System documentation and user manuals; and
11. Training/orientation for authorized end-users, subject to institutional arrangements.

X. Conclusion

The proposed Integrated Registrar–Cashier System is envisioned not only as a solution to improve existing administrative processes but also as an opportunity to demonstrate the capability and relevance of the Information Technology program in addressing actual institutional needs.

Through the collaboration of the IT program, thesis students, and concerned campus offices, the project may contribute to the digital transformation, service efficiency, and institutionalization of locally developed information systems at BISU – Balilihan Campus.

It is therefore respectfully proposed that the project be considered as a potential IT thesis/capstone project and be endorsed to qualified students for further requirements analysis, system design, development, testing, and possible implementation.
