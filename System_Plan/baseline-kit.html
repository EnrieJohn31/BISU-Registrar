Baseline observation kit

How the Registrar's and Cashier's offices work today, measured on paper before the system exists · BISU Registrar, Balilihan Campus · Version 1, 18 October 2026

Why we measure the "before"

  • The thesis claims the system makes service faster and clearer. A claim needs a "before" number to compare with.
  • Real waiting times, peak hours and turnaround days let us set honest targets for the queue, reports and server size instead of guessing.
  • It is cheap now and impossible later, because once the system runs the old way is gone.

What it takes

  • 5 working days in each office, including one day the office calls busy.
  • Two timed hours a day (morning and afternoon) at the public counters, plus a tally of arrivals.
  • Counts and dates copied from the office's own records, by or with a staff member.
  • 2 observers per office are comfortable; 1 is enough on a quiet day.
Privacy rules. These come before anything else.
  • No names. No ID numbers. No receipt numbers. No photos, video or audio. Not even "just for a moment".
  • Each client is a row number and a transaction code. Nothing on the sheets can identify a person.
  • The office head signs the permission form (page 2) before anyone observes. Inform the Data Protection Officer and the adviser as well.
  • Do not interview or ask clients anything. Stand where staff tell you. Never look at staff screens or papers.
  • If anyone, staff or client, asks you to stop, stop and tell the project lead.

How to observe

Read this once, and keep it with the sheets.

The three clock times write them to the minute (HH:MM) from your phone clock

TimeThe moment to record
ArrivedThe client joins the line or waiting area, or takes a number if the office uses one. If you first notice them already waiting, write the time you noticed and tick "late" in Notes.
Service startThe client reaches the counter or window and the staff member begins to attend to them.
Service endThe client steps away from the counter after the transaction is done.

Waiting time = start minus arrived. Service time = end minus start. We calculate them later; you only write the clock times.

Each day do these in order

  1. Before you start: check the signed permission form is on file. Fill in the top of Sheet D (the day sheet).
  2. Agree the transaction codes with the office. Ask staff for the five most common transactions and write them at the top of Sheet A (Type 1 to 5). Everything else is "Other". Use the office's words, not ours. Keep the same codes all week.
  3. Tally (Sheet A), whenever you are present: for each person who arrives, make one mark in the right column and the right 30-minute row. One mark per person, not per document.
  4. Timed windows (Sheet B): twice a day, one hour each: the first hour of the morning and of the afternoon, or the office's own peak hour if it names one. Follow every client who arrives, one row each. If more than 12 arrive in 30 minutes, follow every second arrival and tick that box on the sheet.
  5. Records (Sheet C): with the office's permission, copy the dates and document types, as the form describes. Only once per office.
  6. At closing: finish Sheet D, and check that every timed row has its times or an outcome.

Outcome codes and special cases

Outcome (Sheet B)

  • S served: the transaction was done at the counter.
  • L left before service: they arrived and left without reaching the counter. Leave the start and end blank.
  • R referred: sent to another office or window. Note which one if you can hear it without asking.

Special cases

  • Someone comes back later the same day: a new row.
  • Someone cuts in or is served out of turn: record the real times, and write "priority" in Notes if staff say it is a priority lane.
  • The counter closes for a break: note it in Sheet D; do not stop the clock for the client.
  • You are unsure of a time: write your best estimate and put "?" in Notes.

After each day within one day, while you remember

  1. Type every row into the matching CSV template (provided with this kit), with the same column names. Fix typing mistakes, but never change a recorded time to look tidier.
  2. Add up Sheet A and compare the total with the office's own daily count, if it keeps one. Note any large difference.
  3. Give the paper sheets to the project lead. They hold no personal data. Keep them until the thesis is accepted, or destroy them earlier if the office asks. The "after" measurements will repeat exactly this method.
